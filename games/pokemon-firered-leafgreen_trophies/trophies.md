## Notes

Originally released for Game Boy Advance in 2004; this list targets the digital Nintendo Switch / Switch 2 re-release dated February 27, 2026 (two separate $19.99 SKUs, Pokémon Day/30th anniversary). Genesis Mode: Nintendo platforms have no native achievement/trophy system, and FireRed/LeafGreen have never released on a platform that has one. All Switch-specific mechanical claims in the research dossier rest on secondary gaming press rather than an official Nintendo patch-notes document; treated as medium confidence throughout. Scope decisions, including corrections made after the first audit pass: (1) FRLG-024 (Kanto Pokédex completion) targets the real, in-game-defined 150 species (Mew excluded) rather than an invented lower number. Several of those 150 are version-exclusive wild encounters (e.g. the Vulpix/Growlithe and Oddish/Bellsprout-family splits) and can only be obtained by trading with a second physical Nintendo Switch/Switch 2 running the opposite version over local wireless — this has been inherent to FireRed/LeafGreen's design since the original 2004 cartridges, independent of this platform, and the dependency is disclosed directly on that trophy rather than hidden. (2) A 'catch a Shiny Pokémon' trophy was deliberately NOT included: this generation's flat 1/8192 odds have no Shiny Charm, Masuda Method, or chaining mechanic to offset them, so the chance of one occurring in a normal playthrough is not reasonable under Rule 21. (3) All other multiplayer-dependent content is excluded per Rule 13: the two non-chosen starters and the four trade-evolutions (Kadabra→Alakazam, Machoke→Machamp, Graveler→Golem, Haunter→Gengar) require a second physical Switch and a second copy of the game over local-wireless-only play (no online trading exists on this release, even on Switch 2); the Dodrio Berry Picking minigame is similarly multiplayer-only. None of these are referenced by any trophy. (4) FRLG-016 (Trainer Tower) is framed around beating each mode's default record time within the time limit — the facility's actual completion condition — rather than merely winning a single battle there; it avoids naming which of the four reward items maps to which mode, since that specific mapping is only single-sourced in research. (5) FRLG-019 (Keeper of the Tickets) exists specifically to spotlight the one genuine platform-specific addition found in research: the Mystic and Aurora Tickets are now auto-granted after the player's first Hall of Fame entry, making Ho-Oh, Lugia, and Deoxys reachable through normal play for the first time (previously real-world-event-distribution-only on the original GBA cartridges). (6) FRLG-023 (Mewtwo) deliberately avoids citing an exact Ruby/Sapphire gem requirement for unlocking Cerulean Cave, since research could not resolve whether one or both gems are required — its verification instead references the broader Sevii Islands Network Machine questline. (7) The Rocket Warehouse antagonist's name ('Gideon' in a single low-quality source) could not be confirmed against a primary source and is not used in FRLG-015's text. (8) The Cinnabar Island Mansion's Team Rocket presence could not be confirmed as an active combat beat distinct from its burned-notes lore content, so no trophy is built around it. (9) The Bug-Catching Contest (a Johto-only feature), Voltorb Flip (a Gen IV remake feature), and Pokémon HOME connectivity were all confirmed absent from this game/release and are not referenced anywhere in the list.
